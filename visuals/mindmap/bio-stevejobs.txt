Steve Jobs (1955–2011) co-founded Apple, reshaped personal computing, animation, music, phones and tablets, and died of pancreatic cancer at 56.
Early life. Born in San Francisco on February 24, 1955, and adopted by Paul and Clara Jobs of Mountain View, California. He grew up near the emerging Silicon Valley, met Steve Wozniak in high school, and dropped out of Reed College in 1972 after one semester, though he kept auditing classes such as calligraphy.
Founding Apple (1976–1985). Jobs and Wozniak started Apple in his family's garage in 1976. The Apple II (1977) was a commercial success, and the company went public in 1980. He led the Macintosh project, launched in 1984. After a power struggle with CEO John Sculley, he left Apple in 1985.
NeXT and Pixar (1985–1997). He founded NeXT, which made advanced workstations, and bought the computer graphics division of Lucasfilm, renamed Pixar. Pixar released Toy Story (1995), the first fully computer-animated feature film, and Disney later bought it in 2006, making Jobs Disney's largest individual shareholder.
Return to Apple (1997–2011). Apple bought NeXT in 1997, and Jobs returned, becoming CEO in 2000. Under him came the iMac (1998), iPod (2001), iTunes Store (2003), iPhone (2007), App Store (2008) and iPad (2010). Apple grew from near bankruptcy into one of the world's most valuable companies.
Personal life and death. He married Laurene Powell in 1991 and they had three children; he also had a daughter, Lisa, from an earlier relationship. He was diagnosed with a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor in 2003, had a liver transplant in 2009, and resigned as CEO in August 2011. He died on October 5, 2011.
Legacy. He is remembered for insisting on tight integration of design, hardware and software, for his product launches, and for his demanding management style.
